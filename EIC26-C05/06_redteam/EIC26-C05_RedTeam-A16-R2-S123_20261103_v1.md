# Red-team review of EIC26-C05 A16-R2, Sections 1–3

Reviewed: `EIC26-C05-A16-R2_S1_Technology_EN.md`, `_S2_Market_EN.md`, `_S3_Team_Finance_Implementation_EN.md` (about 9,400 words before references) and the CN internal notes, against the A16-R1 review of the same date. The executive summary is not part of R2 and still carries the R1 wording.

## Verdict

**R2 fixes the largest problems from the R1 review.**
- **IP is now strong.** There are two Savery-filed families: FR2508386 with its PCT extension, and EP26301484.7. The 2019 family is shown as pending in Savery's name. Claim features are stated, and the Kiel/Fraunhofer CAU process patents are now in the screen.
- **There is one revenue story.** The invented €0.6/1.2/1.8m C05 curve is gone, and the 2027–28 revenue is explained as reference deliveries of the existing platform.
- **The payback is now shown honestly** as a sensitivity table.
- **Gender** has an organisation-wide target with an owner and a date.
- **The vague LOI sentence is gone.**

**It is still short of GO from all three evaluators on all three criteria.** Five issues remain. Each can be fixed tomorrow morning, but three of them need facts only you hold.

1. **The headline result is a single number with no stated basis.** "Machine cooling COP up to 20 at 60 °C waste heat" is the only measured result in the proposal. It has no denominator, no test conditions, no capacity, no test lab and no sorbent, and your own internal notes say the test report has not been located. Any expert will ask what COP 20 divides by. Thermal COP of a single-stage sorption cycle is below 1, so it can only be electrical, but the text never says so.
2. **The market section has no market size in euros.** The TAM/SAM/SOM table is entirely qualitative, and the market-development criterion will be scored on numbers.
3. **The unit's rated capacity is deliberately left undefined.** The text says "the 100 kW normalisation also does not assign a new rated capacity". Every €/kW figure, the payback and the €900/kW target therefore have no stated physical basis. If the €120k package is smaller than 100 kW, the economics fail.
4. **The payback table works against the proposal.** Eight of its ten cells miss the 24–36-month objective, and the proposal adds nothing to offset that. The capacity value that makes the purchase case is mentioned and then explicitly excluded.
5. **About 41 sentences are defensive or internal**, and about 12 of them refer to earlier drafts or internal process. An evaluator would read these as answers to objections nobody raised. The list is below.

**10:00 gate.**
- **IP: pass.** This assumes the EPO receipt for EP26301484.7 and the French/PCT filing receipts exist as described. R2 read the EPO receipt directly. The FR/PCT identity comes from the attorney's invoice, so check the official RO/FR receipt and write the PCT number in its official form (PCT/FR2026/0xxxxx).
- **TRL: conditional.** Truthfully stating TRL 6 now rests on the 2025 COP 20 test. If you can produce the report, or at least its title, date, lab, conditions and the COP definition, the gate passes. If not, the TRL claim rests on management assertion again.

## Blocking items and fixes

**B1. Define COP 20 or it will hurt more than help.**
- Write it as "electrical COP" (cooling output / electrical input of fans, pumps and controls) only if the report confirms that.
- Give the rating point: inlet air temperature and humidity ratio, supply air state, airflow, cooling capacity in kW, electrical input, regeneration heat flow and temperature, and test duration.
- Name the test lab and date, and say whether it was the independent ISO/IEC 17025 test the 2025 ESR (E1) described as under way.
- Add a typical value from the same campaign next to the peak. "Up to 20" alone reads as a single best point.
- State the sorbent in the tested machine. If it was not CAU, say so and frame CAU as a qualified contactor upgrade, which R2 already does structurally.
- The same report should give the unit's rated capacity, which resolves B2.

**B2. State the rated cooling capacity of the product unit.** The €120k package (€110k equipment) has to correspond to a kW figure, or €1,100/kW cannot be checked. If a unit is 100 kW, say so. Then 2032 volume = 1,498 units ≈ 150 MW/year. That matches the 2025 ESR's "€900/kW at ≥150 MW/year", a consistency evaluators will reward and that currently goes unused.

**B3. Re-present the customer economics.**
- Keep the sensitivity table, but cut it to the cases that define the target customer:
  - a grid-constrained data centre, with or without warm-water liquid cooling, at 8,000 h;
  - the cited local tariff;
  - the €1,200/kW and €900/kW cases.
- Add capacity value as a cited scenario, not an "invented cash saving". In a power-constrained facility, the 21–23 kW freed per 100 kW of cooling (EER 3 → 8–10) can carry IT load. Price it at a published wholesale colocation rate for the market (CBRE or a similar source, €/kW/month). That is a sourced number, not an invention. At typical FLAP-D rates it is of the same order as the energy saving, which brings constrained-site payback inside 24–36 months.
- Label it clearly as applying only where the grid connection is the binding constraint.
- For reference, at EER 20 (if COP 20 is electrical) and 8,000 h, payback is 54 / 38 / 29 months at €0.15 / 0.20 / 0.25 per kWh.

**B4. Give the CAU process-patent disposition in one sentence.**
- US10696704B2 claim 1, as quoted, reads on aqueous synthesis of an isophthalate framework below 1.5 bar, which is CAU-10-H chemistry.
- Naming it without a disposition invites the question the 2025 evaluator E1 asked. State which applies, after counsel confirms:
  - material bought from a licensed or rights-holder supplier;
  - a synthesis route outside the claim;
  - a licence under discussion with the holders (only if true);
  - not in force in the manufacturing country.
- Replace "Structural similarity between materials and scientific-board membership determine neither a general licence requirement nor a general exemption" with that sentence.

**B5. Name the heat source; this also fixes the C05 argument.**
- 50–70 °C heat is not available from conventional air-cooled data halls, whose return air is roughly 30–45 °C. It is available as return water from warm-water direct liquid cooling of AI/HPC racks (ASHRAE W45/W+ classes; medium confidence on exact temperatures).
- If that is the HPE configuration, say so. It explains the HPE partnership, makes the regeneration heat genuinely surplus (answering R2's own caution about displaced compressor condenser heat), and defines the product duty as the residual air-side load that liquid cooling leaves.
- It also supports a modest, energy-balance-only C05 statement: the site releases less heat by the compressor work avoided (about 210–230 kW per MW of cooling), using heat its dry coolers would otherwise reject. Do not claim an urban air-temperature reduction.

**B6. Put a number in the SAM.**
- Formula: SAM (€/yr) = annual new-build and retrofit data-centre cooling capacity in Europe and North America (MW, cited CBRE/IEA) × share with a suitable heat source and air-side duty (for example liquid-cooled AI/HPC halls, with a cited share) × €1,200/kW.
- Then show 2031–2032 company revenue as a percentage of it. A share in the low single digits is credible. Anything above about 10% needs a reason.
- If the 2025 Part B had a SAM with a source (the ESR refers to a 0.2–0.6% share), reuse its denominator.
