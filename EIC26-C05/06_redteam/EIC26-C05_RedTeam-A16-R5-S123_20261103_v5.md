## Verdict

R5 is factually sound and template-complete. The core numbers are consistent across all three sections:
- COP 20 measured, 8–20 installed;
- 208–283 kW/MW released;
- 25–35 months / 35–48% ROI;
- €15 bn / 1.2%;
- the €2.49m grant and €2m investment.

Its remaining weakness is narrative, not fact. The text explains processes well but under-sells what an evaluator scores. Five gaps:

1. **§1.1 never says why MOFs make this possible.** The deep-tech core is missing: step-shaped water isotherms allow regeneration at 40–70 °C. Figure 2 shows exactly that, but no sentence explains it. Template 1.1 asks "how your innovation is deep-tech".
2. **The "Alternatives" table (S2 l.61–66) gives instructions ("compare…") instead of advantages.** Template 2.1 asks why customers will adopt.
3. **§2.3 Broader impact has no numbers.** Per MW the product saves 1.7–2.3 GWh, about 330–450 t CO₂ and 4,800–7,200 m³ of water a year, and none of this is stated.
4. **§3.1 does not answer the template's incentive question** ("how are they incentivised (e.g.: ESOP plan)"). The scientific board is named without credentials.
5. **§3.2 has no investor traction and no "catalyse within 6 months to 2 years" sentence.** The template asks both.

About 25 sentences still carry process bookkeeping or defensive qualifiers. They are listed in section 3 with replacements.

## 2. Additions (final wording)

**2a. Deep-tech core. Insert after S1 l.9.**

> The deep-tech core is the sorbent. CAU-series metal-organic frameworks take up water in a sharp step at a defined relative humidity, whereas silica gel takes it up gradually and zeolites bind it so strongly that they need much hotter regeneration (Figure 2). Staging materials with decreasing step positions along the air path, and removing adsorption heat inside the same exchanger, lets a small temperature swing release most of the adsorbed water. That is why 40–70 °C waste heat is enough to regenerate the sorbent.

**2b. Comparison table. Replaces S2 l.59–68.**

> Operators facing a heatwave on a constrained connection have four practical alternatives:
>
> | Alternative | Limitation in a heatwave | AirTransformer |
> |---|---|---|
> | Compression chiller with free cooling | Free cooling is unavailable in hot hours; EER falls to about 3 | Cooling without compression at installed COP 8–20 |
> | Desiccant wheel with compression post-cooling | Higher regeneration temperature; post-cooling still draws compressor power | Regeneration at 40–70 °C; evaporative post-cooling |
> | Evaporative cooling alone | Capacity limited by humid inlet air | Pre-dried air lowers the wet-bulb limit, so evaporative cooling works on humid days |
> | AirJoule Prime / Core DH | Built for water production and industrial dehumidification | Delivers a cooling duty to the facility |
>
> AirTransformer's staged heat-and-mass exchanger is covered by Savery's pending 2025 application family. [S2-4]

**2c. Broader impact in numbers. Replaces S2 l.118 and l.120.**

> Each MW of AirTransformer cooling operating 8,000 hours a year saves 1.7–2.3 GWh of electricity against compression at EER 3. That is about 330–450 t CO₂ at an EU grid intensity of 0.2 kg/kWh, plus 4,800–7,200 m³ of external make-up water. The cooling cycle uses no fluorinated refrigerant. Field qualification measures these effects at equal cooling output, using each site's climate, operating schedule and grid factor.

**2d. Scientific board with credentials. Replaces S3 l.9.**

> Norbert Stock and Aline Auroux sit on Savery's scientific board. Professor Stock (Kiel University), whose group developed the CAU series of metal-organic frameworks, advises on the CAU route, process windows and material performance. Dr Auroux (CNRS, IRCELYON), a specialist in adsorption calorimetry, advises on adsorption and thermodynamic measurement.

**2e. Incentives. Insert after S3 l.46. Your decision: use only if true.**

> Key employees will be incentivised through an option plan set up at the Seed round.

If no plan is intended, write instead how the team is incentivised (equity, bonus or other). The template asks this explicitly, and leaving it blank costs marks.

**2f. Why EIC, and leverage. Insert after S3 l.76.**

> Private hardware investors rarely fund the qualification of a new cooling technology before field evidence exists. The EIC grant carries that qualification risk, and the EIC investment anchors the €8 m Seed round, catalysing the €6 m from other investors within six months to two years of the award. Savery has raised «€ amount» from «number» investors through BSA-AIR instruments.

## 4. Executive summary and challenge-fit statement (final, R5-aligned)

These are still to be added at merge. Use these versions.

> **Executive summary.** Extreme heat now hits Europe's cities when grids and water supplies are already stretched. Data centres cannot pause: they must keep cooling when compression chillers lose efficiency and grid headroom disappears. AirTransformer cools with surplus heat instead of a compressor.
>
> A staged metal-organic framework (MOF) heat-and-mass exchanger dries the incoming air and is regenerated with waste heat: 50–70 °C from liquid-cooled racks, with liquid-water recovery, or 40–50 °C from industrial sources such as air-compressor circuits. An indirect evaporative stage then cools the dried air. The cooling process uses no vapour compression, so fans and pumps are the main electrical loads. Savery's complete machine is at TRL6: in its 2025 EN test it delivered sensible cooling at an electrical COP of 20 with 60 °C waste heat. Installed, COP ranges from 8 to 20, against 3 for compression cooling in a heatwave, so each MW of cooling releases 208–283 kW of grid capacity. A liquid-cooled data centre recovers its investment in 25–35 months at €0.25/kWh.
>
> The product comprises 20 kW modules and 600 kW containers. Savery's pending applications protect the staged exchanger (FR2508386 and its PCT extension, 2025) and a sorption heat pump whose heating COP can reach 6–10 (EP26301484.7, 2026). Norbert Stock and Aline Auroux sit on its scientific board. An awarded CETP project (2026–2029) is taking the system to TRL7 at an HPE site.
>
> The EIC action makes the machine an industrial product: qualified CAU contactor batches, reliability and conformity evidence, field operation and TRL8 by March 2029. Savery's plan reaches €180.9 m revenue in 2032, about 1.2% of a €15 bn annual data-centre cooling opportunity. We request a €2,485,583 grant (70% of €3,550,833 eligible cost) and €2 m EIC investment within an €8 m Seed round closing in November 2027.

> **Challenge fit (after the topic choice).** AirTransformer applies to Deep Tech for Climate Adaptation under its priority of combating extreme heat in urban environments through urban cooling. It is a sorption cooling machine that runs on 40–70 °C waste heat instead of a compressor, with a sensible-cooling COP of 20 in its 2025 EN test. During heatwaves it keeps critical urban facilities cool, releases 208–283 kW of grid capacity per MW of cooling and cuts external make-up water by 40–60%. The action delivers a manufacturable product qualified at TRL8, with performance maps that operators and Mission regions can use to deploy it.
