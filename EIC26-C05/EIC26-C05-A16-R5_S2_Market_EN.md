# 2. Market

## 2.1 Market opportunity

### Cooling continuity in liquid-cooled data centres

AirTransformer's first customers are operators of liquid-cooled data centres and other data centres with suitable waste-heat interfaces that need dependable cooling during extreme heat while managing restricted electricity connections and water availability. Their purchasing decision combines useful cooling, service continuity, electrical capacity, external water demand, installation disruption and support over the equipment's life. An efficient machine creates commercial value when those benefits are delivered at the facility interface and can be accepted by the operator.

Savery's funded CETP programme, with HPE providing the site, offers an established integration and field-development path. The commercial strategy uses this experience to address the existing AirTransformer platform in Europe and North America. The site collaboration is a technical foundation; sales depend on each purchaser's application, decision process, acceptance and contract.

Demand growth is accompanied by infrastructure constraints. CBRE's June 2026 report records year-on-year inventory growth of 33% across the four largest North American markets and 18.9% across the four largest European markets in Q1 2026. It identifies power availability and grid infrastructure as constraints on development and location. These observations support a customer need for cooling capacity that makes better use of the electrical connection already available. The inventory figures measure data-centre infrastructure capacity. [S2-1]

### Grid headroom during a heatwave

For 1 MW of useful cooling, a reference compression system at EER3 draws 333.3 kW. At installed electrical COP8–20, AirTransformer draws 125–50 kW and releases approximately 208–283 kW of cooling electrical capacity, a 62.5–85.0% reduction within the equal-duty comparison:

ΔP = Qcool × (1/EERreference − 1/COPinstalled).

| Installed cooling COP used in the comparison | Electrical input for 1 MW cooling | Electrical capacity released against EER3 | Reduction |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 125.0 kW | 208.3 kW | 62.5% |
| 10 | 100.0 kW | 233.3 kW | 70.0% |
| 20 | 50.0 kW | 283.3 kW | 85.0% |

This engineering comparison uses the company installation range, not a new 1 MW field test. It provides useful headroom on a constrained connection during hot conditions; allocation to additional IT load depends on the site's remaining electrical and operating constraints. Regeneration heat remains a separate input. Site screening checks that rack or industrial heat remains available during cooling, rather than relying on condenser heat from a compressor that the new duty would displace. Site energy and water balances establish the resulting resource and heat-rejection effects.

### Customer value and annual cash return

For the primary liquid-cooled data-centre case, available rack heat regenerates the sorbent without a marginal heat-purchase charge. At €0.25/kWh electricity and €120,000 installed capital per 100 kW of normalised useful cooling, the COP8–20 engineering range gives approximately 25–35 months simple payback and 35–48% annual simple pre-tax cash ROI. The operating assumptions are set out below.

| Customer scenario, per 100 kW normalised cooling | Annual net cash saving | Simple payback | Annual simple pre-tax cash ROI |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Main case: €120k installed; €0.25/kWh | approx. €42–58k | approx. 25–35 months | approx. 35–48% |
| Lower tariff: €120k installed; €0.20/kWh | approx. €33–46k | approx. 31–43 months | approx. 28–39% |
| Future cost scenario: €100k installed; €0.20/kWh | approx. €33–46k | approx. 26–36 months | approx. 33–46% |

Assumptions for all rows: Q = 100 kW useful cooling, H = 8,000 effective hours/year of displaced compression cooling, reference EER3, installed COP8–20, available waste heat at zero marginal heat-purchase cost, baseline external water 1.5 L/kWh-cooling, make-up reduction of 40–60%, water €4/m³ and incremental service €2,000/year. These are effective displacement hours, not facility uptime; free cooling and the site's actual duty change the compression cooling displaced and therefore the return. The range combines the COP and water-saving endpoints. Annual water value is €1,920–€2,880. Financing, taxes, degradation and major replacement are outside this simple cash comparison.

Annual net cash saving = H × Q × (1/3 − 1/COP) × electricity tariff + annual water value − €2,000. Annual cash ROI = net cash saving / installed capital; payback in months = 12 / ROI, with ROI expressed as a fraction.

The €100k future cost scenario uses €900/kW hardware plus €100/kW installation/engineering for this 100 kW normalisation. It leaves the company forecast's €110k equipment, €8k installation and €2k engineering package unchanged and does not assign a 100 kW rating to a forecast equipment unit. Electrical headroom and continuity provide additional customer value outside the cash saving above.

### Alternatives and differentiation

Operators can retain compression equipment, extend free cooling, improve existing water management or procure another sorption or desiccant solution. The comparison must use the useful service the customer needs and the alternative it could actually install.

| Alternative | Customer value and comparison with AirTransformer |
|---|---|
| Compression cooling with available economisation | Established service and support; compare hot-condition electrical demand and the actual annual free-cooling schedule |
| Conventional desiccant air treatment | Moisture control; compare regeneration, adsorption-heat removal, post-cooling and full air-service electricity |
| AirJoule Prime / Core DH | Supplier offerings for water production and industrial dehumidification using low-grade heat; compare the specified service and installation conditions with AirTransformer's assigned cooling duty |
| Retrofit of existing heat rejection or water systems | May improve resource use with lower integration change; compare the residual cooling limitation, full installed cost and service benefit |

AirTransformer differentiates through a cooling duty delivered without vapour compression, an installed COP8–20 engineering range, and staged heat-and-mass-exchanger design covered by Savery's pending 2025 application family. Comparisons with AirJoule's water-production and dehumidification offerings use matched service and installation conditions. [S2-4]

### Commercial terms and installed-base service

Planned billing is 20% on order, 20% on completion, 55% on acceptance and 5% after a six-month retention period. The central collection assumption is one month after invoicing. The delivery target is nine months, with a proposed twelve-month refund long-stop. These terms make acceptance quality and field capacity central to cash generation. [S2-3]

Maintenance starts twelve months after acceptance. The plan assumes 60% attachment, a fee equal to 3% of equipment value per year and 90% renewal. Costs include spares, contracted support, field personnel and warranty obligations. The maintenance offer will define response, inspection, replacement and operating responsibilities for the released configuration.

Ina Wang leads account development and customer requirements with engineering support. The investment programme adds a planned HVAC/data-centre business-development lead from M12, then application and service capacity as the fleet grows. Account reviews track source/sink compatibility, evidence required for the purchaser's decision, scope, authority, timing and delivery capacity.

## 2.3 Broader impact

The direct adaptation outcome is continued useful cooling for urban digital infrastructure during extreme heat, with reduced pressure on constrained electricity and water resources. Data centres support communications, enterprise activity and public-service delivery; maintaining their assigned cooling service helps those users withstand climate stress. The product is evaluated at a defined duty so that a machine result can be translated into an operational benefit.

Electrical impact is measured at equal cooling output, including affected auxiliary loads. Annual energy impact is assembled from the actual climate and operating schedule. Where a site has lower-carbon electricity at some hours, emissions assessment uses the applicable temporal and geographic factors and the regeneration-heat counterfactual.

Water impact is measured as lower external make-up per unit of cooling and lower withdrawals during constrained periods. The acceptance protocol separates external supply, recovered moisture, losses and changes in stored water. Use of recovered liquid follows the actual treatment and application requirements.

European value creation includes thermal engineering, material processing, contactor manufacture, assembly, metrology, product release and lifecycle service. The action plans 258 person-months across fourteen execution roles. Qualification instructions, trained deputies and supplier transfer packages support repeatable skilled work. Larger employment and industrial expenditure follow the delivered fleet and factory programme; they are tracked separately from project effort and current headcount.

The aluminium-based CAU route, standard metal heat-exchanger construction and planned European material processing, contactor manufacture and final assembly provide practical routes to reduce supplier dependence. Savery will qualify alternative sources for critical inputs and preserve process specifications, acceptance tests and change records so that production can transfer between suitable suppliers. Component-level provenance and material checks will guide sourcing and identify remaining non-European or critical-material dependencies.

Customer energy requirements also encourage adoption. In Germany, EnEfG §11 requires data centres entering service from 1 July 2026 to reach PUE ≤1.2 as a sustained annual average within two years of commissioning. Lower cooling electricity can contribute to that whole-facility objective; compliance depends on the complete data-centre design and operation. [S2-7]

Circularity work addresses replaceable contactors, access for service, component life and recoverable materials. Manufacturing and lifecycle records will identify consumables and treatment requirements as well as equipment. Assessment compares the complete useful service and its resource inputs, allowing improvements in durability or maintainability to be considered alongside initial efficiency.

## 2.4 Challenge alignment (FOR CHALLENGES ONLY)

AirTransformer addresses the C05 priority of combating extreme heat in urban environments through urban cooling. Heatwaves raise cooling demand while compression efficiency, grid headroom and water availability can deteriorate together. Using suitable surplus heat for cooling, the product targets continuity of assigned cooling service with less electricity and external make-up water. The Challenge explicitly includes urban cooling among its adaptation priorities. [S2-5]

| Metric | Action target or comparison | Verification |
|---|---|---|
| Electricity at equal useful cooling | Existing 60–70% reduction qualification target; ≥60% lower boundary | Matched-service tests including affected auxiliaries |
| Grid headroom per MW cooling | 208–283 kW engineering comparison at installed COP8–20 against EER3 | Electrical input and delivered cooling over the qualified map |
| External make-up water at equal cooling | Existing 40–60% reduction target | Metered external water and full site water balance |
| Cooling continuity in heatwave conditions | Assigned duty maintained within the qualified operating envelope | Climate/load campaigns and the existing field programme |

Performance maps and site balances also record regeneration heat and useful recovered liquid across the climate and load conditions used for acceptance.

Urban liquid-cooled data centres come first because they combine persistent cooling duty with suitable 50–70°C rack heat and constrained electricity connections. CETP and HPE provide an established integration pathway; the EIC increment makes the equipment manufacturable, reliable and supportable through batch quality, conformity, industrial controls and product qualification. Qin and the field lead will secure incremental EIC access and operating responsibilities before M12, as set out in Section 3.

The 40–50°C operating mode without liquid-water recovery also opens suitable industrial waste-heat and urban critical-facility applications. Savery will use the 600 kW container configuration, documented performance maps and installation specifications to engage infrastructure operators through the Adaptation Mission's regional and local-authority networks. Expansion follows qualification of each facility's heat source, sink and cooling duty.

## 2.5 STEP Impact (ONLY FOR CHALLENGES WITHIN THE SCOPE OF STEP)

The European contribution is a controllable product and manufacturing capability in advanced sorption materials, integrated thermal equipment and industrial software. Savery's 2025 exchanger family, separate 2026 system filing and process know-how support European design authority. The EIC work converts that authority into batch specifications, production and release methods, reliability evidence and a transferable service package.

Supply resilience comes from identifying critical materials and components, qualifying sources, documenting changes and retaining the knowledge needed to maintain and reproduce the equipment. Transfer arrangements define access to drawings, processes and improvements. Software and data dependencies are recorded so that product support can continue as suppliers and configurations evolve.

Savery will track critical development, processing, assembly, testing and service activity by location and expenditure. These measures connect strategic European capacity to work actually performed. The Europe-and-North-America sales plan remains a company commercial plan; EU manufacturing, skills and installation outcomes are identified from the corresponding activities and deployments.
