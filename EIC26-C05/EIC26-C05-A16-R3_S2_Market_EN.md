# 2 Market

## 2.1 Market opportunity

### Cooling continuity in electricity-constrained data centres

AirTransformer's first customers are data-centre owners and operators that need dependable cooling during extreme heat while managing restricted electricity connections and water availability. Their purchasing decision combines useful cooling, service continuity, electrical capacity, external water demand, installation disruption and support over the equipment's life. An efficient machine creates commercial value when those benefits are delivered at the facility interface and can be accepted by the operator.

Savery's funded CETP programme, with HPE providing the site, offers an established integration and field-development path. The commercial strategy uses this experience to address the existing AirTransformer platform in Europe and North America. The site collaboration is a technical foundation; sales depend on each purchaser's application, decision process, acceptance and contract.

Demand growth is accompanied by infrastructure constraints. CBRE's June 2026 report records year-on-year inventory growth of 33% across the four largest North American markets and 18.9% across the four largest European markets in Q1 2026. It identifies power availability and grid infrastructure as constraints on development and location. These observations support a customer need for cooling capacity that makes better use of the electrical connection already available. The inventory figures measure data-centre infrastructure capacity. [S2-1]

Market access is established through a common site screen. The account must have an identified cooling requirement and a viable installation position; a heat source that remains available in the proposed operating mode; a compatible sink; suitable air and water interfaces; and an operator able to accept and support the service. Commercial qualification adds budget authority, purchasing timetable, contractual responsibilities and an installation window. Only an account that passes these steps enters a capacity-backed delivery commitment.

Savery's serviceable business is the Europe-and-North-America subset passing this technical and commercial screen. Its obtainable-market plan follows accepted equipment and associated service revenue in Section 2.2, aligned with acquisition, manufacturing, installation and support capacity.

### Electrical capacity during a heatwave

At a fixed useful cooling duty, reducing electrical input can release capacity for other facility loads or reduce the additional connection capacity needed. For electrical cooling performance EER, the calculation is:

ΔP = Qcool × (1/EERreference − 1/EERAirTransformer).

For 1 MW of useful cooling and a reference EER of 3, reference electrical demand is 333.3 kW:

| AirTransformer electrical performance used in the scenario | Electrical input for 1 MW cooling | Electrical-power difference from EER3 | Reduction within this comparison |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| EER8 | 125.0 kW | 208.3 kW | 62.5% |
| EER10 | 100.0 kW | 233.3 kW | 70.0% |
| EER20 | 50.0 kW | 283.3 kW | 85.0% |

These are equal-duty calculations. The EER20 row represents transfer of the reported maximum COP20 machine point only where the test's useful-cooling and electrical-input definitions match this electrical comparison. Regeneration heat is a separate input. Installation auxiliaries and residual plant duties are included before a site electrical-capacity benefit is contracted. The figures do not describe a measured 1 MW installation or annual average performance.

The purchasing value can be strongest during the hours when a high cooling load coincides with a constrained electrical connection. A reduction of approximately 208–233 kW per MW of assigned cooling in the EER8–10 scenarios is material to that decision even when annual electricity savings vary with climate and operating mode. The COP20 scenario indicates a larger potential at a compatible point, to be established over the customer's operating map.

Heat availability is screened with the same care. A source must supply usable heat at the required temperature and duration while the proposed cooling system operates. If the proposed source is condenser heat from a compressor that AirTransformer would displace, its continuing availability cannot be assumed. The design must identify another source, retained operation or a different duty allocation and include its consequences in the resource balance.

The quantified adaptation benefit is the cooling service retained and electrical headroom created under declared conditions. A reduction in compressor electricity alone does not establish the change in total site heat rejection or an urban air-temperature reduction. Those outcomes require a complete site energy balance, including the origin and alternative disposal of regeneration heat.

### Annual economics and operating-mode sensitivity

Annual savings depend on the hours and loads for which compression cooling is actually displaced. Free-cooling periods, partial-load operation, humidity, heat availability and source/sink conditions determine those hours and the effective installation efficiency. Customer screening therefore uses operating-mode and load data, rather than applying the maximum test COP to every hour of the year.

The screening calculation below uses a 100 kW useful-cooling normalisation, €120,000 installed capital, reference EER3 and effective electrical EER10 for the displaced duty. The normalisation is independent of the rated capacity of a forecast equipment unit. It assumes regeneration heat of 100 kW during those hours, priced at €0.01/kWh, water savings worth €0.30 per operating hour and incremental annual service cost of €2,000. The heat assumption is equivalent to thermal COP1; it is independent of electrical COP20. Water value assumes a 1.5 L/kWh-cooling reference, 50% make-up reduction and €4/m³. These are screening assumptions, with actual thermal and water inputs determined for the site.

| Electricity tariff | Simple payback at 4,000 effective displaced hours/year | Simple payback at 8,000 effective displaced hours/year |
|---|---:|---:|
| €0.20/kWh | 103.8 months | 48.4 months |
| €0.25/kWh | 77.7 months | 36.9 months |
| €0.30/kWh | 62.1 months | 29.8 months |
| €0.35/kWh | 51.7 months | 24.9 months |

The net annual benefit in this example is H × [(100/3 − 100/10) × p − 1.00 + 0.30] − 2,000, where H is effective displaced hours and p is the electricity price in €/kWh. Simple payback is installed capital divided by that benefit. Financing, tax, degradation and major replacement are outside this screening calculation.

At 8,000 effective displaced hours and €0.30–0.35/kWh, this example meets the 24–36-month commercial objective. Lower utilisation or electricity prices lengthen payback, as the table shows. Electrical-capacity and continuity benefits are assessed separately from the cash savings calculated here.

### Alternatives and differentiation

Operators can retain compression equipment, extend free cooling, improve existing water management or procure another sorption or desiccant solution. The comparison must use the useful service the customer needs and the alternative it could actually install.

| Alternative | Customer value and comparison with AirTransformer |
|---|---|
| Compression cooling with available economisation | Established service and support; compare hot-condition electrical demand and the actual annual free-cooling schedule |
| Conventional desiccant air treatment | Moisture control; compare regeneration, adsorption-heat removal, post-cooling and full air-service electricity |
| AirJoule Prime | Current supplier offering for industrial water production with dry air, using low-grade heat; water-production metrics do not establish a matched cooling-COP comparison |
| AirJoule Core DH | Separate industrial-dehumidification offering; compare its specified humidity-control service and integration requirements with the assigned AirTransformer duty |
| Retrofit of existing heat rejection or water systems | May improve resource use with lower integration change; compare the residual cooling limitation, full installed cost and service benefit |

AirJoule distinguishes Prime water production from Core DH industrial dehumidification. Savery's differentiation is the integrated cooling duty, demonstrated machine performance, staged heat-and-mass-exchanger engineering and qualification at customer interfaces. Comparison will use each product's specified service, matched operating conditions and full installation costs; water-production efficiency and cooling COP retain their respective definitions. ## 2.3 Broader impact

The direct adaptation outcome is continued useful cooling for urban digital infrastructure during extreme heat, with reduced pressure on constrained electricity and water resources. Data centres support communications, enterprise activity and public-service delivery; maintaining their assigned cooling service helps those users withstand climate stress. The product is evaluated at a defined duty so that a machine result can be translated into an operational benefit.

Electrical impact is measured at equal cooling output, including affected auxiliary loads. Annual energy impact is assembled from the actual climate and operating schedule. Where a site has lower-carbon electricity at some hours, emissions assessment uses the applicable temporal and geographic factors and the regeneration-heat counterfactual. Reduced electricity demand does not by itself establish a complete carbon result.

Water impact is measured as lower external make-up per unit of cooling and lower withdrawals during constrained periods. The acceptance protocol separates external supply, recovered moisture, losses and changes in stored water. Use of recovered liquid follows the actual treatment and application requirements. This supports credible resource performance without relying on an assumed daily water-production rate.

European value creation includes thermal engineering, material processing, contactor manufacture, assembly, metrology, product release and lifecycle service. The action plans 258 person-months across fourteen execution roles. Qualification instructions, trained deputies and supplier transfer packages support repeatable skilled work. Larger employment and industrial expenditure follow the delivered fleet and factory programme; they are tracked separately from project effort and current headcount.

Circularity work addresses replaceable contactors, access for service, component life and recoverable materials. Manufacturing and lifecycle records will identify consumables and treatment requirements as well as equipment. Assessment compares the complete useful service and its resource inputs, allowing improvements in durability or maintainability to be considered alongside initial efficiency.

## 2.4 Challenge alignment

AirTransformer addresses the C05 priority of combating extreme heat in urban environments through urban cooling. The application combines a sorption-based deep-tech machine with material and heat-exchanger engineering, using suitable low-temperature heat to reduce the electrical and external-water burden of an assigned cooling duty. Urban data centres are the first customer setting because high cooling demand can coincide with heatwaves, restricted grid connections and water stress. The Challenge explicitly includes urban cooling among its adaptation priorities. [S2-5]

The adaptation chain is concrete: an operator identifies a heat-exposed cooling duty; application engineering verifies source, sink and interfaces; the product supplies that duty under the relevant conditions; and acceptance measures cooling continuity, electricity and external water. The EER8–10 calculations show approximately 208–233 kW/MW of potential electrical headroom against the stated EER3 reference. A compatible COP20 test-point scenario gives approximately 283 kW/MW. These calculations guide site evaluation, while contracted performance follows qualification of the installation.

CETP and HPE provide an existing integration pathway. The EIC increment makes the equipment manufacturable and supportable through batch quality, reliability, conformity, industrial controls and product acceptance. Independent climate and endurance tests provide evidence for conditions that cannot be captured during the planned field window. This supports an adaptation product that can be repeated at further qualified sites.

The company will use documented performance maps, installation requirements and service cases to support engagement with the Mission's regional and local-authority networks. Such engagement can connect infrastructure operators, heat sources and local resource constraints. Hospitals, campuses and other urban facilities are potential later applications where their duties and interfaces qualify; no municipal customer or additional site is assumed.

## 2.5 STEP impact

The European contribution is a controllable product and manufacturing capability in advanced sorption materials, integrated thermal equipment and industrial software. Savery's 2025 exchanger family, separate 2026 system filing and process know-how support European design authority. The EIC work converts that authority into batch specifications, production and release methods, reliability evidence and a transferable service package.

Supply resilience comes from identifying critical materials and components, qualifying sources, documenting changes and retaining the knowledge needed to maintain and reproduce the equipment. Transfer arrangements define access to drawings, processes and improvements. Software and data dependencies are recorded so that product support can continue as suppliers and configurations evolve.

Savery will track critical development, processing, assembly, testing and service activity by location and expenditure. These measures connect strategic European capacity to work actually performed. The Europe-and-North-America sales plan remains a company commercial plan; EU manufacturing, skills and installation outcomes are identified from the corresponding activities and deployments.
