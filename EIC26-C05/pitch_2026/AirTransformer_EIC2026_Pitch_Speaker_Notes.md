## Script

**1. AirTransformer** (ends at about 0:27)

Good morning. I am Frédéric Cui, founder and CEO of Savery Tech. AirTransformer turns the low-temperature heat that data centres already reject into cooling, without a compressor. Our complete machine is at TRL6 and measured an electrical COP of 20 with 60-degree waste heat. With the EIC, we will qualify it as a manufacturable TRL8 product by March 2029.

**2. Heatwaves push cooling into grid and water limits** (ends at about 1:05)

Cooling is where climate stress hits digital infrastructure. In a heatwave, compression cooling falls to an EER of about 3: a third of a megawatt of electricity for every megawatt of cooling, at the hours when the grid is tightest. Power availability already limits where data centres can be built, evaporative systems draw water just when drought restrictions apply, and Germany now requires new sites to reach a PUE of 1.2. Meanwhile, liquid-cooled racks reject 40 to 70-degree heat that nobody uses.

**3. Rack heat in, cooling out: no vapour compression** (ends at about 1:40)

AirTransformer uses that heat. Staged MOF contactors dry the process air, and waste heat regenerates them. The dry air then passes a cooling heat exchanger, where water evaporates into a separate working-air stream without mixing. There is no vapour compression: electricity only drives fans, pumps and controls. The machine reached COP 20 in our 2025 EN test, and 8 to 20 installed. The product is a 20-kilowatt module, assembled into 600-kilowatt containers beside the existing cooling.

**4. Material, cycle and machine engineered as one** (ends at about 2:16)

The breakthrough is not one material; it is matching material, contactor, cycle and control. Our aluminium-based CAU MOFs from Kiel University switch their water uptake in steep, low-temperature steps, and each stage uses a different step as the air dries. Coated fins and fluid tubes remove adsorption heat as it forms: that is our 2025 patent family. Finite-time design, from my published work with Henninger, sets switching and regeneration, and a model-predictive controller runs inside hard safety limits.

**5. Ten years of prototypes, one complete TRL6 machine** (ends at about 2:54)

Ten years of iteration: three proofs of concept and three prototypes, from 1 kilowatt in 2019 to 25 kilowatts in 2024, led to a complete TRL6 machine. In the 2025 EN test it delivered sensible cooling at an electrical COP of 20 with 60-degree heat. Across 20 to 40 degrees ambient and 30 to 70 percent humidity, COP stays between 14 and 19: performance holds in the heat. Site comparisons include every pump and auxiliary, so we quote 8 to 20 installed.

**6. What changed since the 2025 evaluation** (ends at about 3:30)

Since 2025, we have answered both points the evaluators raised. On IP, every family is now filed by Savery Tech, and our freedom-to-operate work names the rights we map. On the team, two engineers and an interim CFO have joined, both boards are in place, and we target at least 40 percent women among all new hires. We raised 400 thousand euros through BSA-AIR, won the CETP programme with HPE Labs, and focused on one product and one beachhead.

**7. Savery owns its IP; FTO follows the CAU route** (ends at about 4:10)

Savery owns three patent families. The 2025 family protects staged MOFs with decreasing adsorption thresholds and integrated heat exchange, the core of the product, with a PCT extension filed in July. The 2026 European application covers a sorption heat pump with routable vapour circulation, our heating extension. Freedom to operate follows the exact CAU route we manufacture: we have mapped BASF, NuMat, Framergy, AirJoule and the Kiel-Fraunhofer process patents, and each disposition is recorded at the M12 gate, before manufacturing transfer. Process know-how stays in-house.

**8. A €15bn market, entered through liquid-cooled sites** (ends at about 4:46)

We plan against a 15-billion-euro data-centre cooling opportunity; independent analysts put the global market at 20.5 billion dollars. We start where heat, grid limits and liquid cooling meet: urban liquid-cooled data centres in Europe and North America, with 50 to 70-degree rack heat. Four reference units with two prospective customers come in 2027. By 2032 we plan 1,498 units and 181 million euros of revenue, about 1.2 percent of our planning reference. Industrial 40 to 50-degree waste heat follows.

**9. Cooling from waste heat, without compressor or vacuum** (ends at about 5:24)

Customers compare us with four alternatives. Compression with free cooling loses its free hours in a heatwave. Desiccant systems still need mechanical post-cooling. Closed-cycle adsorption chillers use vacuum vessels and make chilled water that still needs air handling. Water-harvesting and dehumidification systems such as AirJoule are built for water or drying. AirTransformer treats the air directly at atmospheric pressure and delivers the cooling duty without vapour compression, with water recovery as a mode. That staged architecture is what our 2025 family protects.

**10. Per MW: 208–283 kW of grid headroom in a heatwave** (ends at about 6:03)

Per megawatt of cooling, compression at EER 3 draws 333 kilowatts; AirTransformer draws 50 to 125. That frees 208 to 283 kilowatts of grid capacity at the hottest hours, and saves 1.7 to 2.3 gigawatt-hours and 4,800 to 7,200 cubic metres of water a year. For the customer, at 25 cents per kilowatt-hour, a 120-thousand-euro unit per 100 kilowatts pays back in 25 to 35 months. This is the C05 outcome: urban cooling that keeps running in extreme heat with less electricity and water.

**11. Sold on acceptance, grown by replication** (ends at about 6:42)

We sell equipment, installation and engineering at 120 thousand euros per unit, then maintenance at 3 percent of equipment value per year. Billing is tied to acceptance, so delivery quality drives cash. Each acquisition pod pairs an account executive with an application engineer, and an acceptance at one site leads to more units there, then further sites and regions. Our channel ecosystem covers design offices, installers, distributors, maintainers and operators. Units grow from 4 in 2027 to 1,498 in 2032, paced by factory and field capacity.

**12. TRL6 machine to qualified TRL8 product in 24 months** (ends at about 7:22)

The EIC action runs from April 2027 to March 2029: 258 person-months across five work packages. It closes the gap between a working machine and a product: controlled manufacture, reliability, conformity and field acceptance. Every gate has a number. By M18, six qualified batches and 1,200 independent fixture-hours; by M21, 4,000 endurance hours and conformity release; at M24, 1,600 field hours and verified savings of at least 60 percent electricity and 40 percent water, our TRL8 decision. The investment work package then readies serial production.

**13. The team to build, qualify and sell the product** (ends at about 7:57)

I lead architecture and product, from a background in sorption, thermal modelling and industrial transfer. Qin leads engineering and production; Ina leads sales with 15 years of B2B experience; Nicolas is our interim CFO. Two engineers are in post, and eleven planned roles map to work-package needs. Our board brings two Corps des Mines engineers and a data-centre executive; our scientific board brings Professor Stock, whose Kiel group created the CAU materials, Dr Auroux and Driss Stitou.

**14. €75m revenue in 2031 as factory capacity scales** (ends at about 8:31)

Revenue grows from 0.5 million euros in 2027 to 75 million in 2031 and 181 million in 2032. EBITDA stays negative while we build factory and field capacity. We need about 95 million euros to reach operating break-even, and the Seed, Series A and Series B raise 101 million net. Cash stays positive in every month. The EIC grant co-finances costs that are already in our business plan; it adds no new spending.

**15. The ask: €4.49m to qualify the product and scale** (ends at about 9:10)

We ask the EIC for 4.49 million euros: a 2.49-million grant, 70 percent of the 3.55-million action, and 2 million in equity. The equity anchors an 8-million Seed with 6 million from other investors, launching in September 2027 and closing in November. Of the EIC investment, 1.9 million goes to manufacturing deployment and service capacity and 100 thousand co-finances the action. The EIC is the right partner because qualification risk comes before revenue, and EIC participation signals to investors that this risk is being retired.

**16. Waste heat in, resilient cooling out** (ends at about 9:36)

To close: heatwaves, grid limits and efficiency rules are hitting data centres now, and liquid cooling has made their waste heat usable. We have the machine, the IP and the team. With the EIC, we turn a COP-20 machine into a qualified European product that keeps urban digital infrastructure cool in extreme heat. Thank you.
